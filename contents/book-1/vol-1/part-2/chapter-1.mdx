Clarity is not simplification at the expense of truth. Clarity is removing every word that stands between the reader and the actual mechanism.

### The Three Enemies of Clear Technical Prose

1. **Nominalizations**: Turning crisp verbs into heavy nouns ("perform an initialization of" instead of "initialize").
2. **Unanchored Pronouns**: Starting a sentence with "This enables..." when the preceding paragraph named three different components.
3. **Hidden Prerequisites**: Referring to a configuration flag before telling the reader where that configuration lives.

### Sentence-Level Revision Checklist

Compare before and after revision:

- **Before**: "In the event that the connection pool experiences exhaustion, a timeout exception will be thrown by the client."
- **After**: "When all pooled connections are busy past the timeout window, the client throws a `PoolTimeoutError`."

Notice how the revised sentence preserves full technical precision while cutting word count by 40% and naming the concrete error class.

### Precision Over Politeness

Avoid filler lead-ins such as "It is important to note that..." or "Simply run the command below." If a step matters, state the consequence directly. If a step is genuinely delicate, tell the reader why instead of calling it simple.
